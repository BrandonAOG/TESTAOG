📋 Gas Pipe Bonding Reference
Plain-language summary of NFPA 54 (2024) 7.12, not the official code text.
7.12.1 — PIPE & TUBING OTHER THAN CSST
Above-ground piping likely to become energized must be electrically continuous and bonded to an effective ground-fault current path. It counts as bonded when it's connected to an appliance that's connected to the equipment grounding conductor of its circuit.
7.12.2 — CSST
A system with any CSST must be bonded to the electrical service grounding electrode system (or the lightning protection grounding electrode system, where there is one).
WHY CSST GETS ITS OWN BOND
Nearby lightning (not just a direct hit) energizes all the metal in a house unevenly. When the voltage difference between the CSST and nearby metal (wiring, water pipe, ductwork) gets big enough, it arcs and can burn a hole in the thin CSST wall. The 6 AWG bond ties the CSST to the grounding electrode system so everything stays at about the same voltage. Summarized from the 2021 NFPA 54 Handbook, Supplement 6.
WHAT THE RESEARCH FOUND (GTI, 2013)
• Clamp location in the system was not a significant factor. That's why the code allows any rigid pipe, fitting or CSST fitting.
• Shorter jumpers work better. Jumpers up to 150 ft still prevented perforation. The code set 75 ft to be conservative.
• Extra jumpers at more locations didn't measurably help. One bond is enough.
• Arc-resistant CSST bonded through the EGC performed about the same as yellow CSST with the 6 AWG bond, so the code treats it like steel pipe. Summarized from the 2021 NFPA 54 Handbook, Supplement 6.
7.12.2.1 — WHERE TO CLAMP
The jumper connects to a metallic pipe, pipe fitting or CSST fitting, not the corrugated tubing.
7.12.2.2 — WIRE SIZE
Not smaller than 6 AWG copper or equivalent. Larger is allowed. Solid or stranded is fine (Annex A.7.12.2).
7.12.2.3 — LENGTH
Jumper no longer than 75 ft from the gas piping connection to the grounding electrode system. If it would be longer, add a grounding electrode closer and bond it to the service grounding electrode system. Keep it as short as practical (A.7.12.2.3). Shorter is better: the length matters more than where the clamp goes.
7.12.2.4 / 7.12.2.5 — CONNECTIONS
Connections per NFPA 70. Clamps and devices listed to UL 467.
A.7.12.2 — LANDING POINTS (ANNEX, INFO ONLY)
Service equipment enclosure, grounded conductor at the service, grounding electrode conductor (if big enough), or directly to a grounding electrode. Lightning protection grounding electrode if shorter, never a down conductor. Clamps should stay accessible after construction. State and local laws may limit who can attach to the building grounding system.
7.12.3 — ARC-RESISTANT CSST
Must be listed arc-resistant to CSA/ANSI LC 1/CSA 6.26. It's treated like steel pipe: bonded through the appliance equipment grounding conductor. If any CSST in the system isn't arc-resistant, 7.12.2 applies to the whole system.
7.12.4 — ELECTRICAL ISOLATION
Gas piping is never a grounding conductor or electrode. Underground metal piping gets a dielectric fitting at the building penetration, and dielectric fittings are never installed underground.
7.12.5 — LIGHTNING PROTECTION
Where a lightning protection system is installed, bond the gas piping per NFPA 780.
7.13 — NOT A CIRCUIT
Gas piping can't be used as a circuit conductor. Exception: control, ignition and flame-detection circuits of 50 V or less.
PROPANE — NFPA 54 vs NFPA 58
NFPA 54 covers LP piping from the outlet of the final pressure regulator to the appliances (54 1.1.1.1(A)), so 7.12 bonding applies there. NFPA 58 covers the tank side and says grounding and bonding aren't required on LP-Gas systems (58 6.26.1.3). That's static-electricity bonding: the gas is in a closed system (A.6.26.1.3). NFPA 58 doesn't apply to the parts NFPA 54 covers (58 1.3.2(6)).
FLORIDA / MANUFACTURER
Florida natural gas is enforced under the Florida Building Code – Fuel Gas, which has its own CSST bonding section. Also follow the CSST manufacturer's installation guide, which can add requirements. Verify with the AHJ.
Plain-language summary of NEC 2023 250.104(B), not the official code text.
250.104(B) — OTHER METAL PIPING (INCLUDING GAS)
Metal piping in or attached to a building that's likely to become energized must be bonded to one of:
(1) the equipment grounding conductor of the circuit likely to energize it
(2) the service equipment enclosure
(3) the grounded conductor at the service
(4) the grounding electrode conductor, if big enough
(5) a grounding electrode, if its conductor is big enough
SIZING
Bonding jumper sized from Table 250.122, using the rating of the circuit likely to energize the piping. Attachment points must be accessible.
HOW IT FITS WITH NFPA 54
Steel pipe or arc-resistant CSST on a grounded appliance is bonded through that appliance's EGC (option 1). Standard CSST needs the dedicated 6 AWG bond to the grounding electrode system in NFPA 54 7.12.2, which is more than 250.104(B) alone asks.
250.104(B) INFO NOTES
Bonding all piping and metal air ducts adds safety. See NFPA 54 and NFPA 780 for gas piping.
NEC 2023 Table 250.122. Size a 250.104(B) bond from the breaker of the circuit likely to energize the piping.
