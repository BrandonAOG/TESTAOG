INFORMATIONAL NOTE — PULLING
Table 1 assumes common conditions: proper cabling and alignment of conductors, with reasonable pull lengths and number of bends. Some conditions may need a larger raceway or a lower fill.
INFORMATIONAL NOTE — JAM RATIO
Pulling three conductors or cables: if conduit ID ÷ conductor OD is between 2.8 and 3.2, the conductors can jam. With four or more, jamming is very unlikely. This is the jam check this calculator already shows.

Plain-language summary of the Notes to Chapter 9 tables, checked against NEC 2023 — not the official code text. Verify with the adopted code & AHJ.
NOTE 1 — SAME-SIZE CONDUCTORS
For conductors all the same size, Informative Annex C lists the maximum number allowed in each conduit type and size.
NOTE 2 — WHERE TABLE 1 APPLIES
Table 1 applies to complete conduit or tubing systems, not to short sleeves used only to protect exposed wiring from physical damage.
NOTE 3 — GROUNDS COUNT
Equipment grounding and bonding conductors must be included in the fill, using their actual dimensions (insulated or bare).
NOTE 4 — NIPPLES
A nipple 24" or shorter (connectors not counted in the length) between boxes, cabinets or similar enclosures may be filled to 60%, and the 310.15(C)(1) adjustment factors for more than three current-carrying conductors don't apply to it.
NOTE 5 — CONDUCTORS NOT IN THE TABLES
For conductors not covered in Chapter 9 — such as multiconductor cables and optical fiber cables — use their actual dimensions.
NOTE 6 — MIXED SIZES
For a combination of conductor sizes, use the actual dimensions or Table 5 / 5A for the conductors, and Table 4 for the conduit — which is how this calculator works.
NOTE 7 — ROUNDING UP
When figuring the max number of same-size conductors or cables, if the result's decimal is 0.8 or more, round up to the next whole number. Same idea for sizing conduit for a single conductor: a result of 0.8 or more counts as one conductor.
NOTE 8 — BARE CONDUCTORS
Where bare conductors are allowed, their dimensions from Table 8 may be used.
NOTE 9 — CABLES & CORDS
A multiconductor cable, optical fiber cable, or cord with two or more conductors counts as one conductor for Table 1. For an oval (elliptical) cable, use its major diameter as the circle diameter.
But: single insulated conductors bundled together without an overall jacket are not a cable — count each conductor individually.
NOTE 10 — TABLE 5 VALUES
Table 5 diameters and areas are worst-case values for round, concentric-lay stranded conductors; solid and stranded share values. Round compact-stranded values are in Table 5A. If the actual diameter and area are known (e.g. from the manufacturer), you may use them.
